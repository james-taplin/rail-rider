# Four-rat resetting gallery

Original model, based on commercially available resettable airgun galleries. The outer sheet-metal shroud is approximately 0.50 m wide × 0.205 m deep × 0.205 m tall. Each complete rat plate is approximately 92 mm across including its tail; the circular reset is 38 mm in diameter. Rat bodies and the reset have nearly equal centre height (125 mm and 124 mm respectively). The opening faces forward; a roof, solid end caps, rear casing and bottom close the housing. The internal backstop rises 190 mm over a 190 mm run, giving a 45° slope. These are modelling estimates informed by the references, not measurements of an exact commercial product. One Blender unit and one glTF unit are one metre.

The front lip bears the requested lettering `DAD'S RESET-ABLE RAT REVUE`. The generator retains the exact text; in the Blender source and exported GLB the lettering is converted to a mesh.

- `rat_gallery.blend` is the editable source. The collection `EXPORT_GALLERY` contains the prop; the floor, lighting and camera are presentation objects and are not in the GLB.
- `rat_gallery.glb` contains named meshes and pivot nodes, original embedded material textures, and nine independent clips. The four independent rat hinges are visibly supported by the continuous fixed `Common rat support axle`. For rat N, `Rat_0N_Down` moves `Rat_0N_Pivot` from standing to fallen; `Rat_0N_Up` reverses that motion. `Reset_Hit` rotates the full paddle (stem, circle and centre stud) away from the incoming shot around the lower `Reset_Pivot`, reaches the common axle at the −24° peak, rebounds past rest by 5°, and settles. The keyed contact is at frame 5, about 0.2083 seconds in the exported 0.5-second clip. The reset clip can play on every hit, even when no rats are down. A viewer or future game integration must track which targets are fallen, retain their poses, and play Up clips only on fallen targets after a reset hit. There are no simulated mechanisms, collision shapes or game behaviour in this asset.
- `rat_gallery_still.png` is a front three-quarter still. `rat_gallery_animation.gif` demonstrates two falling, a partial reset, then all four falling and resetting. The interactive viewer is the authoritative way to inspect each angle and sequence.
- `build_gallery.py` constructs the source, GLB and still in Blender. `render_gallery_preview.py` renders the numbered frames for the companion animation without overwriting the source. `reset_contact_side.png` is a diagnostic side view at the contact pose with housing and rat silhouettes hidden to reveal the stem and axle.

The grey-black steel, silver target coating, pivot points, folded edges and deflector are visual interpretation rather than an exact reconstruction. In the front projection, the circle has at least 6.6 mm clearance from the neighbouring silhouettes; the furthest target edge during a 90° drop has approximately 16 mm clearance to the angled backstop by construction. `check_clearance.py` sampled each rat at every degree over its 90° fall and the reset paddle over −24° to +5°: no mesh overlaps were found with the axle, backstop, roof or front lip. The nominal analytic gap between the paddle's rear face and the axle at peak is about 0.006 mm, placing the keyed meshes essentially in contact without interpenetration. This is a sampled mesh check, not continuous collision proof or physical simulation. Nothing here has been integrated into Rail-Rider or the game.
